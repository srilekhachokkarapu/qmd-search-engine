# Paracetamol (Acetaminophen)

## 1. Overview

Paracetamol, called acetaminophen in the United States, is a non-opioid analgesic and antipyretic medicine. It is commonly used for temporary relief of mild to moderate pain and reduction of fever. It does not have the same meaningful peripheral anti-inflammatory effect as NSAIDs such as ibuprofen.

This document is for general education and information retrieval. It is not a prescription or an individualized treatment plan.

## 2. Drug Classification

- Therapeutic class: Analgesic and antipyretic
- Drug family: Non-opioid pain reliever
- Generic names: Paracetamol; acetaminophen

## 3. Common Uses

Paracetamol may be used for temporary relief of:

- Headache
- Toothache
- Mild muscle aches
- Backache
- Menstrual pain
- Sore-throat discomfort
- Fever associated with common illnesses
- Mild pain after some procedures, when recommended by a clinician

It treats symptoms; it does not treat the underlying cause of an infection or other illness.

## 4. Mechanism of Action

Paracetamol reduces pain and fever through actions in the central nervous system. Its precise mechanism is not fully understood. It is thought to influence central prostaglandin-related pathways involved in pain signalling and temperature regulation.

It has relatively little peripheral anti-inflammatory activity at usual therapeutic use.

## 5. Available Dosage Forms

Depending on the country and manufacturer, products may include:

- Tablets and caplets
- Capsules
- Oral liquid or suspension
- Dispersible or soluble tablets
- Suppositories
- Intravenous formulation used in clinical settings

Strengths and approved age ranges vary by product.

## 6. Use and Administration Principles

- Use only the dose and interval stated on the specific product label or prescribed by a clinician.
- Check the active ingredients of cold, flu, headache and combination pain products.
- Do not take more than one paracetamol-containing product at the same time unless instructed by a clinician.
- Do not exceed the product's stated maximum daily amount.
- For children, dosing is product- and weight-dependent.
- Ask a clinician before use if you have liver disease, low body weight, malnutrition or regular heavy alcohol use.

This reference does not provide a universal dose because product strengths and national labelling differ.

## 7. Common Side Effects

Possible unwanted effects include:

- Nausea or stomach discomfort
- Skin rash or itching
- Rare allergic reactions

A severe skin reaction or signs of an allergic reaction require urgent medical assessment.

## 8. Serious Risks and Warnings

### Liver Injury and Overdose

Taking too much paracetamol can cause severe liver injury, liver failure and death.

Overdose may be accidental, especially when a person combines several cold, flu or pain products that contain the same ingredient.

If an overdose is possible, seek emergency medical advice immediately, even if the person feels well. Early symptoms may be absent or mild. Do not wait for symptoms.

### Allergy and Severe Skin Reactions

Seek urgent medical care for:

- Breathing difficulty
- Facial or throat swelling
- Widespread blistering or peeling rash
- Other signs of a severe allergic reaction

## 9. Contraindications and Precautions

Obtain professional advice before use if you:

- Have liver disease or a history of liver problems
- Regularly drink substantial amounts of alcohol
- Are malnourished or have very low body weight
- Take other medicines that may affect the liver
- Are pregnant or breastfeeding and are unsure whether the product is appropriate
- Are considering giving it to an infant or child

## 10. Drug Interactions

Potentially important interactions include:

- Warfarin: Regular use of paracetamol may affect anticoagulation control; monitoring may be needed.
- Other paracetamol-containing products: Duplicate ingredients can cause accidental overdose.
- Certain medicines that affect liver enzymes or liver function may alter risk.

Always show the clinician or pharmacist the full list of medicines and supplements.

## 11. Missed Dose

For occasional use, follow the package directions.

If prescribed on a schedule and a dose is missed, follow the patient leaflet or ask a pharmacist. Do not double a dose to make up for a missed one.

## 12. Storage and Handling

- Keep in the original labelled packaging.
- Store according to package instructions.
- Keep away from excess heat and moisture.
- Keep out of the reach and sight of children.
- Do not use expired or damaged products.

## 13. Patient Education Checklist

Before using paracetamol, confirm:

1. The exact active ingredient and strength.
2. Whether another medicine already contains paracetamol.
3. The correct product-specific dose and interval.
4. Whether liver disease or alcohol use creates additional risk.
5. What to do if an overdose may have occurred.

## 14. When to Seek Medical Help

Seek urgent help for suspected overdose, breathing difficulty, facial or throat swelling, severe rash, yellow skin or eyes, or severe abdominal symptoms.

Obtain medical advice if fever or pain is persistent, worsening or recurrent.

## 15. Key Facts for Document Search

- Paracetamol is also called acetaminophen.
- It is an analgesic and antipyretic.
- It is used for pain relief and fever reduction.
- Overdose can cause severe liver damage.
- Combination cold and flu products may contain paracetamol.
- Suspected overdose needs urgent assessment even without symptoms.
