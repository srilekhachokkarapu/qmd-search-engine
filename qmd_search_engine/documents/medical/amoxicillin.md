# Amoxicillin

## 1. Overview

Amoxicillin is a penicillin-family beta-lactam antibiotic. It treats certain infections caused by susceptible bacteria.

It does not treat viral illnesses such as the common cold or influenza.

Antibiotics must be used only when prescribed or otherwise supplied under an authorized local protocol. This document is for education and search-engine testing, not for self-medication.

## 2. Drug Classification

- Therapeutic class: Antibacterial antibiotic
- Pharmacological class: Aminopenicillin; beta-lactam
- Generic name: Amoxicillin
- Route: Usually oral

## 3. Common Clinical Uses

A clinician may prescribe amoxicillin for selected susceptible bacterial infections, which can include:

- Certain ear, nose and throat infections
- Some lower respiratory tract infections
- Selected urinary tract infections
- Certain skin and soft-tissue infections
- Dental infections in specific circumstances
- Helicobacter pylori eradication as part of a combination regimen

The appropriate antibiotic depends on the infection site, likely organism, local resistance patterns, allergy history and patient factors.

Amoxicillin is not appropriate for every infection in these categories.

## 4. Mechanism of Action

Amoxicillin binds to penicillin-binding proteins involved in bacterial cell-wall construction.

It interferes with peptidoglycan cross-linking, weakening the bacterial cell wall and causing susceptible bacteria to die.

It is generally described as bactericidal against susceptible organisms.

Some bacteria produce beta-lactamase enzymes that can inactivate amoxicillin.

A clinician may choose a combination such as amoxicillin with clavulanic acid in selected circumstances. This is a different product and should not be substituted without professional direction.

## 5. Available Dosage Forms

Depending on the market, formulations may include:

- Capsules
- Tablets
- Chewable tablets
- Oral suspension
- Injectable formulations in selected settings

Strengths and formulation instructions vary.

Oral suspensions may require specific reconstitution, storage and discard instructions from the pharmacy label.

## 6. Use and Administration Principles

- Take only when prescribed for the current infection.
- Follow the exact prescribed dose, interval and duration.
- Take doses at evenly spaced times as directed.
- Measure liquid medicine with the supplied oral syringe or measuring device.
- Do not share antibiotics with another person.
- Do not use leftover antibiotics for a new illness.
- If symptoms do not improve or become worse, contact the prescriber.

Follow the prescriber's instructions about completing the prescribed course. Do not independently stop, extend or restart treatment.

## 7. Common Side Effects

Possible side effects include:

- Nausea
- Vomiting
- Diarrhoea
- Stomach discomfort
- Skin rash
- Oral or vaginal yeast infection in some people

Persistent or severe diarrhoea needs medical assessment, including after the antibiotic has been stopped.

## 8. Serious Risks and Warnings

### Penicillin Allergy and Anaphylaxis

Amoxicillin can cause serious allergic reactions.

Hives, swelling of the face or throat, wheezing, difficulty breathing or collapse require emergency medical care.

### Severe Skin Reactions

Blistering, peeling skin, sores in the mouth or eyes, or a widespread severe rash require urgent assessment.

### Antibiotic-Associated Diarrhoea

Antibiotics can disrupt normal gut bacteria.

Severe, persistent or bloody diarrhoea, abdominal cramps or fever may indicate a serious complication such as Clostridioides difficile infection.

Seek medical advice promptly.

### Antibiotic Resistance

Unnecessary or inappropriate antibiotic use contributes to antimicrobial resistance.

Antibiotics do not treat viral infections.

## 9. Contraindications and Precautions

Tell the prescriber or pharmacist if you:

- Have had an allergic reaction to amoxicillin, penicillin or another beta-lactam antibiotic
- Have kidney disease
- Have a history of antibiotic-associated colitis
- Have glandular fever or mononucleosis, because rash can occur
- Are pregnant, planning pregnancy or breastfeeding
- Have a history of significant allergies

Kidney function may affect the dose or interval selected by the prescriber.

## 10. Drug Interactions

Potentially important interactions include:

- Warfarin and other anticoagulants: Monitoring may be needed.
- Methotrexate: Penicillins may affect methotrexate clearance and toxicity risk.
- Allopurinol: May increase the likelihood of skin rash.
- Probenecid: Can increase amoxicillin exposure.
- Other antibiotics: Combinations should be selected by a clinician.

Provide the clinician with a complete medicine and supplement list.

## 11. Missed Dose

Follow the prescription leaflet or ask a pharmacist.

In general, take a missed dose when remembered unless it is nearly time for the next dose. Do not double doses.

## 12. Storage and Handling

- Store capsules and tablets as directed on the packaging.
- Reconstituted liquid may require refrigeration or room-temperature storage depending on the product.
- Follow the pharmacy label for the beyond-use date.
- Follow the label for disposal of unused suspension.
- Shake liquid suspension if instructed.
- Keep medicines out of the reach and sight of children.

## 13. Patient Education Checklist

Before and during treatment:

1. Confirm that the antibiotic was prescribed for the current infection.
2. Tell the prescriber about any penicillin or cephalosporin allergy.
3. Follow the exact schedule and duration.
4. Do not share or save leftover antibiotics.
5. Seek advice for worsening symptoms, severe diarrhoea or a rash.
6. Ask how to store and discard liquid suspension.

## 14. When to Seek Medical Help

Seek emergency care for breathing difficulty, facial or throat swelling, collapse or a severe blistering rash.

Contact a clinician promptly for severe or bloody diarrhoea, persistent vomiting, worsening infection or symptoms that fail to improve as expected.

## 15. Key Facts for Document Search

- Amoxicillin is an aminopenicillin beta-lactam antibiotic.
- It inhibits bacterial cell-wall synthesis.
- It treats selected susceptible bacterial infections.
- It does not treat colds, flu or other viral infections.
- Penicillin allergy and antibiotic-associated diarrhoea are important safety considerations.
- Inappropriate antibiotic use contributes to antimicrobial resistance.
