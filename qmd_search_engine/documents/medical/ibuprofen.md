# Ibuprofen

## 1. Overview

Ibuprofen is a non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID). It is used to relieve pain, reduce fever and decrease inflammation.

It is available in different formulations and strengths, some over the counter and others by prescription depending on the country.

This document is educational, not an individualized treatment recommendation. Ibuprofen is not suitable for everyone.

## 2. Drug Classification

- Therapeutic class: Analgesic, antipyretic and anti-inflammatory
- Pharmacological class: Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID)
- Generic name: Ibuprofen

## 3. Common Uses

Depending on the product and local approval, ibuprofen may be used for:

- Headache and migraine-related pain
- Dental pain
- Menstrual cramps
- Muscle aches and back pain
- Joint pain and inflammatory conditions
- Fever
- Pain and inflammation after minor injury

The reason for pain or fever may need separate assessment.

## 4. Mechanism of Action

Ibuprofen reversibly inhibits cyclo-oxygenase enzymes (COX-1 and COX-2), reducing the production of prostaglandins.

Prostaglandins contribute to pain, fever and inflammation. Reducing their production helps relieve these symptoms.

The same prostaglandin effects also help protect the stomach lining and maintain kidney blood flow. This explains some important NSAID risks.

## 5. Available Dosage Forms

Products may include:

- Tablets and capsules
- Oral liquid or suspension
- Topical gel or spray
- Intravenous formulations used in selected clinical settings

Strengths, age restrictions and directions vary by product and country.

## 6. Use and Administration Principles

- Use the lowest effective dose for the shortest period stated on the label or prescribed by a clinician.
- Some oral products are best taken with or after food to reduce stomach irritation.
- Do not combine ibuprofen with another NSAID, such as naproxen, unless specifically directed by a clinician.
- Check combination cold and flu products for NSAIDs.
- Do not exceed the labelled or prescribed amount.

This document does not give a universal dose because dosing depends on age, formulation, indication and health conditions.

## 7. Common Side Effects

Possible side effects include:

- Indigestion or heartburn
- Stomach pain
- Nausea or vomiting
- Diarrhoea or constipation
- Headache
- Dizziness
- Skin rash

Seek professional advice if side effects are persistent or severe.

## 8. Serious Risks and Warnings

### Gastrointestinal Injury

Ibuprofen can cause stomach or intestinal ulcers, bleeding or perforation.

Warning signs include:

- Severe abdominal pain
- Vomiting blood or material resembling coffee grounds
- Black, tarry stools

### Kidney Effects

NSAIDs can reduce kidney blood flow and may cause kidney injury, particularly in people who are dehydrated or have existing kidney disease or heart failure.

### Cardiovascular Risks

NSAIDs may increase the risk of heart attack or stroke, particularly with higher doses or longer use.

### Allergic and Asthma Reactions

Wheezing, facial swelling, difficulty breathing or a severe rash may indicate a serious reaction.

## 9. Contraindications and Precautions

Ibuprofen may be unsuitable for people who:

- Have had an allergic reaction to ibuprofen, aspirin or another NSAID
- Have or have had a stomach ulcer or gastrointestinal bleeding
- Have significant kidney, liver or heart disease
- Have certain blood-clotting disorders or a history of stroke
- Have asthma that worsens with aspirin or NSAIDs
- Are dehydrated
- Are pregnant or breastfeeding and have not checked suitability with a professional

Pregnancy is especially important: NSAIDs can pose fetal risks at certain stages. Use during pregnancy should be guided by a clinician.

## 10. Drug Interactions

Important potential interactions include:

- Anticoagulants such as warfarin: Increased bleeding risk.
- Other NSAIDs, including naproxen: Increased adverse-effect risk.
- Corticosteroids: Increased gastrointestinal ulcer or bleeding risk.
- Some antidepressants, including SSRIs and SNRIs: May increase bleeding risk.
- Blood-pressure medicines and diuretics: Ibuprofen may affect blood-pressure control or kidney function.
- Lithium or methotrexate: Levels or toxicity may be affected.

This is not a complete interaction list.

## 11. Missed Dose

For occasional use, follow the package directions.

For scheduled prescription use, follow the leaflet or ask a pharmacist. Do not take a double dose to compensate for a missed dose.

## 12. Storage and Handling

- Store in the original packaging according to the label.
- Keep away from excess heat and moisture.
- Keep out of the reach and sight of children.
- Check expiry dates.
- Store topical products securely.

## 13. Patient Education Checklist

Before using ibuprofen, consider:

1. Whether you have a history of ulcers, bleeding, kidney disease or heart disease.
2. Whether you are pregnant or could be pregnant.
3. Whether another medicine increases bleeding risk.
4. Whether you are dehydrated.
5. Whether you are using the lowest effective amount for the shortest necessary time as directed.

## 14. When to Seek Medical Help

Get urgent medical help for:

- Chest pain
- Sudden weakness or speech difficulty
- Vomiting blood
- Black stools
- Severe abdominal pain
- Facial or throat swelling
- Difficulty breathing

Seek medical advice for reduced urination, new swelling or persistent side effects.

## 15. Key Facts for Document Search

- Ibuprofen is an NSAID.
- It reduces prostaglandin production by inhibiting COX enzymes.
- It can relieve pain, fever and inflammation.
- Important risks include stomach bleeding, kidney injury and cardiovascular events.
- It should not be combined with other NSAIDs without professional direction.
- Pregnancy, ulcers, kidney disease and NSAID allergy require particular caution.
